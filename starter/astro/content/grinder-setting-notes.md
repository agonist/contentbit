# Grinder setting notes

These espresso grinder settings turn burr grinder notes into a decision trail.
The number on the dial matters less than the direction, the size of the move,
and whether the next shot tastes closer to balanced.

:::callout{type="tip" title="Move in one language"}
Record grinder changes in the same unit every time: numbers, clicks, or
microns. Mixed notation makes old notes impossible to compare.
:::

## Starting points

:::tabs
::tab{title="Flat burr"}
Start mid-range and move in **single steps**. Flat burrs are unforgiving but
consistent: small moves, big differences.
::tab{title="Conical burr"}
Start two-thirds toward fine. Conicals are gentler near the edges, so you can
move in double steps until you get close.
::tab{title="Hand grinder"}
Count clicks from zero. Most hand grinders land between 9 and 14 clicks for
espresso. Write your number down.
:::

## Adjustment rules

:::pros-cons
+ Finer grind raises pressure and slows the shot
+ Coarser grind lowers pressure and speeds the shot
- Changing dose and grind together hides the cause
- Old beans can mimic a grind problem
:::

:::quick-ref
- Sour and fast | Grind finer
- Bitter and slow | Grind coarser
- Good taste, odd time | Keep it and log it
- Same grind, new beans | Reset the recipe
:::

The [dial-in guide](/blog/dialing-in-espresso) helps you dial in espresso
against a target taste. The
[espresso recipe log](/blog/espresso-recipe-log) keeps each grinder move
connected to the shot that proved it.
