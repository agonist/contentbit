# Espresso recipe log

An espresso recipe log turns coffee notes into a system. The goal is not
paperwork; it is remembering which change made the coffee sweeter, clearer, or
less harsh.

:::comparison{left="Loose notes" right="Recipe log"}
- Recall | Whatever you remember | Dose, yield, time, grind, taste
- Next shot | Guess again | Change one variable
- Sharing | Hard to reproduce | Easy to hand off
- Trend spotting | Nearly impossible | Obvious after a week
:::

## Minimum useful record

:::steps
1. Write the coffee, roast date, dose, yield, shot time, and grinder setting.
2. Taste before editing the recipe.
3. Change only one variable.
4. Mark the best shot so it becomes tomorrow's baseline.
:::

:::faq
::faq-item{question="Do I need to log every shot?"}
No. Treat it as a shot journal for dial-in work and surprisingly good shots.
Skip the routine ones once the recipe is stable.
::faq-item{question="What if the same recipe tastes different tomorrow?"}
Check bean age, humidity, and grinder retention before changing multiple
variables. The grinder notes help isolate the cause.
::faq-item{question="Should taste override shot time?"}
Yes. Time is a guide rail; taste is the decision.
:::
