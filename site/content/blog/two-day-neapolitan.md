Most pizza recipes fail at the same spot: they treat dough as a list of
ingredients instead of a schedule. This guide is the schedule. It takes ten
minutes of work today and gives you blistered, foldable slices tomorrow.

:::callout{type="tldr"}
Mix four ingredients tonight, fold twice, refrigerate. Tomorrow: ball, proof
two hours, stretch, and bake as hot as your oven goes.
:::

:::key-metrics
- 65% | Hydration
- 24h | Cold ferment
- 250g | Ball weight
- 2 min | Bake time
:::

## What you need

No mixer required. A kitchen scale is non-negotiable — volume measures drift
by twenty percent, and dough punishes that.

:::quick-ref
- Flour (00 or bread) | 612g
- Water, cool | 398g
- Fine sea salt | 18g
- Instant yeast | 1.2g
- Makes | 4 × 250g balls
:::

## Choosing your yeast

Fresh yeast has romance; instant yeast has reliability. For a 24-hour cold
ferment the difference in flavor is **smaller than people claim** — time does
the heavy lifting, not the yeast.

:::comparison{left="Fresh yeast" right="Instant"}
- Amount needed | 3.5g | 1.2g
- Where to buy | Bakeries | Any supermarket
- Shelf life | ~2 weeks | ~2 years
- Flavor at 24h | Marginally richer | Clean, neutral
:::

## The method

:::steps
1. Whisk the yeast into the water, then add flour and salt. Mix with one hand until no dry flour remains — about three minutes of mess.
2. Rest 20 minutes, covered. This pause (autolyse) does the early kneading for you.
3. Fold the dough over itself eight times. Rest 30 minutes. Repeat once more.
4. Refrigerate, covered, 18 to 24 hours. The dough will double and smell faintly of beer.
5. Divide into four 250g balls. Proof, covered, 2 hours at room temperature.
6. Stretch from the center out, leaving a 2cm rim untouched. Top lightly and bake immediately.
:::

The two mixing approaches below end in the same place — pick the one that
matches your kitchen.

:::tabs
::tab{title="Stand mixer"}
Dough hook, speed 2, eight minutes. Stop when the dough **clears the bowl**
and windows without tearing. Walk away — overmixing heats the dough.
::tab{title="By hand"}
Wet your hand, slide under the dough, stretch and fold over. Quarter-turn the
bowl, repeat. Four folds every 30 minutes, four rounds total. Slower, same
gluten, and you learn what good dough feels like.
:::

:::callout{type="warning" title="The rim is sacred"}
Press from the center outward and never flatten the outer 2cm. That trapped
gas ring becomes the leopard-spotted cornicione — crush it and you get a
cracker.
:::

## Should you cold-ferment?

A same-day dough is possible. It is also a compromise.

:::pros-cons
+ Far deeper flavor — enzymes need the long, cold night
+ Forgiving schedule: bake anytime in a 12-hour window
+ Easier to stretch; gluten fully relaxed
- You have to plan a day ahead
- Fridge space for a 4-liter container
:::

## Questions everyone asks

:::faq
::faq-item{question="Can I freeze the dough balls?"}
Yes — after dividing. Thaw overnight in the fridge, then give them the full
2-hour room-temperature proof before stretching.
::faq-item{question="My oven only reaches 250°C. Is it hopeless?"}
No. Use a pizza steel, preheat 45 minutes, and run the broiler for the last
two minutes. You trade two minutes of bake time for four — still excellent.
::faq-item{question="Why is my dough tearing when I stretch it?"}
It is under-proofed or too cold. Give the balls 30 more minutes at room
temperature and try again — patience fixes most dough problems.
:::

That is the whole craft: weigh precisely, ferment slowly, bake violently.
Print the quick reference, tape it inside a cabinet, and stop reading recipes.
